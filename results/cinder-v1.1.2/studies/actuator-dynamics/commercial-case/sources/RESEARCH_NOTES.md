# Commercial-case research notes

The Sidewinder search was repeated specifically for smaller helix angles. Dalton's current pre-programmed Sidewinder YSR list bottoms out at **31 deg for a straight helix** and **28 deg as the terminal segment of YSR36/28**. STM lists stock Sidewinder replacements no lower than 33/38 or 34 deg, although it will make custom angles. Therefore 28 deg is the most aggressive sourced pre-programmed Sidewinder segment currently used in this study.

With the CINDER convention

\[
H=\frac{1}{r_h\tan\alpha},
\]

neither 31 deg nor 28 deg automatically matches the Baja motion ratio because the commercial effective helix radius is probably larger. For the frozen Baja values, `H ≈ 61.81 rad/m`. Matching that with 28 deg would require `r_h ≈ 30.4 mm`; matching it with 31 deg would require `r_h ≈ 26.9 mm`.

At the current provisional commercial radius range of 45–60 mm, the OTS Sidewinder helix therefore gets its large reflected inertia mainly from a larger `I_s,M`, not a larger `H`.

The 28 deg local segment is nevertheless useful: under the current high-sensitivity mass/radius estimate it raises the reflected axial inertia from about `28.0 kg` (2.92x Baja) at 31 deg to about `35.8 kg` (3.72x Baja). Reaching 4x Baja with the same high MOI would require an effective 28 deg helix radius of roughly `43.4 mm`, only slightly below the current provisional 45 mm lower bound; 5x would require about `38.8 mm`. Those >3.7x values should not be claimed until the actual roller-track radius is measured.

A genuine E-Z-GO 28 deg driven clutch was also found, but its listed complete-clutch shipping weight is only 5 lb and the application is much lower power, so it is retained only as evidence that 28 deg driven-clutch ramps are commercially real rather than as the preferred high-dynamic example.
